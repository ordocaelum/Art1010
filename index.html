Drawing + Painting + Print List

1. Drawing

Title of Artwork: A study of a woman's hands

Artist Name: Leonardo da Vinci

Year Created: c. 1490

Page Number: 107

Medium: Metalpoint, heightening, and charcoal on paper

This drawing shows two hands that appear to be resting or craddling, gently. The background has faint, loose lines suggesting clothing folds, but the hands are the focus. The composition uses very soft values to show dimension. Instead of heavy dark lines, the shapes are built out of delicate, soft lines that describe the form of the fingers and palms. The artist creates a strong sense of space by using shading that makes the skin look soft and realistic. The overall balance feels very calm because the hands are posed so gracefully.

The piece was made using metalpoint, which means the artist dragged a small metal tip across a coated piece of paper. This leaves very fine, delicate lines that naturally darken into a soft gray over time. Because metalpoint cannot be erased, every line had to be drawn with confidence. To make the hands look rounder, the artist combined the metalpoint with charcoal for deeper shadows and white chalk highlights to catch the light. You can easily see these materials working together where the bright chalk pops on the knuckles and the charcoal adds depth.

I really like how soft and alive these hands look even though they were drawn over five hundred years ago. The choice of metalpoint was perfect because it forces the details to be incredibly small and smooth. If this had been done with heavy ink or thick paint, it would probably lose that gentle feeling. It is amazing how much life can come from just small scrapes and chalk on paper.

2. Painting

Title of Artwork: The Great Piece of Turf

Artist Name: Albrecht Dürer

Year Created: 1503

Page Number: 128

Medium: Bodycolors, heightened with opaque white on vellum

This painting shows a close up view of a small patch of grass and weeds growing out of the muddy ground. Visually there is a lot of variety in shapes, with long straight blades of grass reaching toward the top and wide dandelion leaves resting at the bottom. The artist uses a wide range of green and brown colors to make the plants look natural and alive. The composition feels random at first glance, but there is a clear rhythm in how the tall stalks repeat across the page, creating a busy yet unified look at a tiny piece of nature.

The artist used bodycolors, which is basically an opaque watercolor paint often called gouache, on a smooth material called vellum. Opaque watercolors are different from regular watercolors because they let the artist paint light details on top of dark colors instead of just leaving the white paper exposed. You can see this clearly where the highlights of white paint is layered over the dark green blades of grass to show reflection. The medium allowed the artist to capture tiny details like individual seeds on the stalks.

I chose this painting because, to me wilderness and nature contains an untamed, wild beauty that can be difficult to capture. The opaque paint adds a crispness that makes the scene feel fresh, almost like you could reach out and touch the damp grass. Using this medium turns a boring patch of dirt into something incredibly beautiful and worth staring at.

3. Print

Title of Artwork: Christ Crucified between the Two Thieves

Artist Name: Rembrandt van Rijn

Year Created: 1653

Page Number: 137

Medium: Drypoint

This print shows a very intense and dark scene from the Bible with Jesus on a cross right in the center. The background has scarring lines that look like a dark storm or light rays beaming down. The artist uses a lot of contrast between the bright white light in the center and the completely black areas around the side figures. The whole picture feels very serious and messy. There is a abnormal rhythm from all the tiny shapes of horses and crying people lumped together at the bottom of the crosses.

The print was made using drypoint, which is a type of intaglio printmaking where you scratch lines into a soft copper plate with a sharp needle. When you scratch the metal, it leaves a rough edge called a burr. This burr holds onto a bunch of extra ink, which makes the lines look soft and blurry instead of clean like a normal engraving. You can see this fuzzy texture in the really dark shadows on the sides where the ink got stuck in the rough edges of the plate.

I picked this piece because the scene is powerful and dramatic. The drypoint style fits perfectly because the fuzzy, dark lines make everything feel intense and chaotic, as if it is a snapshot of a nightmare. If the lines were clean and straight, it would look way too neat and boring instead of full of emotion.
